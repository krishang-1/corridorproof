# Validation before selling

Candidate buyer: sender/receiver PSP operations or a corridor operator. Candidate users: investigation analysts and their approvers. Neither willingness-to-pay nor procurement access is validated.

1. Interview both sides of one corridor. Ask how often missing outcomes, shortfalls and rejection/refund cases occur, how evidence travels, who can edit the record, and why the current service desk or Swift product is insufficient.
2. Collect de-identified historical exceptions with consent. Record staff minutes, counterparty contacts, missing-evidence requests, rework, and end-to-end resolution times. Separate rail waiting time from coordination time.
3. Replay cases in shadow mode alongside the current workflow. Require no real payments and no customer identifiers in the prototype.
4. Compare a shared database baseline with independently operated Drunix nodes. Measure integration burden and governance benefits. If the partner accepts a trusted operator, prefer the simpler option.
5. Agree a pass/fail gate with partners before testing: no unsafe decisions in replay, complete evidence attribution, and a meaningful reduction in operator effort without increased unresolved cases. Sample size and thresholds depend on their baseline, not invented pitch numbers.

Subscription and integration fees are a pricing hypothesis. Build a bottom-up opportunity from reachable operators, their annual exception volumes and verified spend. Do not multiply national UPI transaction totals by an assumed fee.
